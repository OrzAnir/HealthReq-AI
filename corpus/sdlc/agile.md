# Agile (Scrum)

Agile methods such as Scrum deliver working software in short iterations (sprints of one to four weeks). Requirements are kept in a prioritised product backlog, the customer gives feedback after every sprint, and the plan is adapted continuously.

Strengths: welcomes changing requirements; delivers value early and often; close collaboration with users; problems surface quickly.

Weaknesses: less up-front documentation; harder to give fixed scope and cost; needs an engaged customer; on its own it may not produce the formal evidence that regulators require.

Choose Agile when requirements are expected to change, fast feedback from users matters, and the team can work closely with stakeholders. On its own it is weaker for regulated, safety-critical systems unless combined with formal verification practices.

Source: SDLC reference notes written for this project, summarising standard software engineering textbook material (Sommerville; Pressman).
