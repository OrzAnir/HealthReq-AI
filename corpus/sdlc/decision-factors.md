# Decision factors for choosing an SDLC

Requirement stability: stable requirements favour Waterfall or V-Model; changing requirements favour Agile or a hybrid.

Regulatory and safety criticality: high criticality favours the V-Model or a hybrid with formal verification and documented traceability.

Security and privacy risk: high risk favours DevSecOps practices and security reviews built into every iteration.

Technical uncertainty and project risk: high uncertainty favours Spiral or early prototyping.

Complexity and size: large, complex systems favour iterative models (Spiral, Agile, hybrid) over a single long Waterfall pass.

Delivery speed and release frequency: frequent releases favour Agile and DevSecOps.

Need for formal verification and documentation: favours V-Model, Waterfall or a hybrid that keeps their documentation.

Consequence of failure: if a failure can harm patients or expose personal health data, verification, validation and human approval gates are required whichever model is chosen.

Source: SDLC reference notes written for this project, summarising standard software engineering textbook material (Sommerville; Pressman).
