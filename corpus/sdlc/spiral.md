# Spiral model

The Spiral model (Boehm) develops the system in repeated cycles. Each cycle has four parts: set objectives, identify and resolve risks (often by building a prototype), develop and test, and plan the next cycle. Risk analysis drives every decision.

Strengths: explicit risk management; prototypes reduce technical uncertainty early; suits large, complex and high-risk projects.

Weaknesses: costly and complex to manage; needs risk-assessment expertise; can be excessive for small or low-risk projects.

Choose Spiral when there is high technical uncertainty or risk, such as new technology, unclear feasibility or a large budget at stake. Avoid it for small, well-understood projects.

Source: SDLC reference notes written for this project, summarising standard software engineering textbook material (Sommerville; Pressman).
