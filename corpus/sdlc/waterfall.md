# Waterfall model

The Waterfall model runs the phases strictly in order: requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment and maintenance. Each phase finishes, and is usually signed off, before the next one starts.

Strengths: simple to plan and manage; clear milestones and documentation at every phase; suits fixed-price contracts and projects that need formal approvals.

Weaknesses: changes are expensive once a phase is closed; working software appears only late in the project; problems in the requirements are often discovered during testing.

Choose Waterfall when requirements are stable, well understood and unlikely to change, the technology is familiar, and extensive documentation and approvals are required. Avoid it when requirements are unclear or expected to change.

Source: SDLC reference notes written for this project, summarising standard software engineering textbook material (Sommerville; Pressman).
